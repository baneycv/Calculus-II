Math 252, 8.4 Trigonometric Substitution

  1. 1.

    Purpose. We now explore integrals that contain square roots in the form

    a2−x2,x2+a2,x2−a2.

    The goal is to eliminate the square root from the problem. To this end, we use the following substitutions and corresponding triangles.

    Strategy

    Trigonometric Substitution (a > 0)

    For integrals involving √(a² − u²), let

    u = a sin z   ⟹   u/a = sin z,   √(a² − u²) = a cos z.

    For integrals involving √(u² + a²), let

    u = a tan z   ⟹   u/a = tan z,   √(u² + a²) = a sec z.

    For integrals involving √(u² − a²), let

    u = a sec z   ⟹   u/a = sec z,   √(u² − a²) = a tan z.

    In each case, choose z so the indicated square root is positive.

    Before using this as an integration technique, we will (1) become familiar with the algebra associated with simplifying these square roots, (2) examine how they induce a right triangle configuration, and then (3) put these ideas together to integrate.

  2. 2.

    Example 1: Suppose x=5⁢sin⁡z. Simplify 25−x2.
    Solution:

    25−x2 =25−(5⁢sin⁡z)2
    =25−25⁢sin2⁡z
    =25⁢(1−sin2⁡z)
    =25⁢cos2⁡z
    =5⁢cos⁡z
  3. 3.

    Example 2: Suppose x=3⁢tan⁡z. Simplify x2+9.
    Solution:

    x2+9 =(3⁢tan⁡z)2+9
    =9⁢tan2⁡z+9
    =9⁢(tan2⁡z+1)
    =9⁢sec2⁡z
    =3⁢sec⁡z
  4. 4.

    Example 3: Suppose x=a⁢sec⁡z. Simplify x2−a2.
    Solution: For x2−a2, let x=a⁢sec⁡z with 0<z<π/2. Then

    x2−a2 =(a⁢sec⁡z)2−a2
    =a2⁢sec2⁡z−a2
    =a2⁢(sec2⁡z−1)
    =a2⁢tan2⁡z
    =a⁢tan⁡z.

    Strategy

    Right Triangle Ratios

    sin⁡z=yrcos⁡z=xrtan⁡z=yx

    adjacent side=9−x2.

    r2=x2+42=x2+16,r=x2+16.

  8. 8.

    Integration Technique

    1. (a)

      Set up the substitution. Choose the trigonometric substitution, find d⁢x, solve for the trig function, and solve for the angle.

    2. (b)

      Draw the triangle. Use the substitution to build the right triangle and solve for the missing side.

    3. (c)

      Simplify the square root. Substitute for x, d⁢x, and the square root.

    4. (d)

      Integrate.

    5. (e)

      Back substitute. Use the triangle to express your answer in terms of the original variable.

  9. 9.

    Example 6: Evaluate ∫d⁢xx2⁢9−x2.
    Solution: Let x=3⁢sin⁡z, d⁢x=3⁢cos⁡z⁢d⁢z, x/3=sin⁡z, and z=arcsin⁡(x/3).

    d⁢xx2⁢9−x2 =3⁢cos⁡z⁢d⁢z(3⁢sin⁡z)2⁢9−(3⁢sin⁡z)2
    =3⁢cos⁡z⁢d⁢z9⁢sin2⁡z⁢9−9⁢sin2⁡z
    =3⁢cos⁡z⁢d⁢z9⁢sin2⁡z⁢9⁢(1−sin2⁡z)
    =3⁢cos⁡z⁢d⁢z9⁢sin2⁡z⁢9⁢cos2⁡z
    =3⁢cos⁡z⁢d⁢z9⁢sin2⁡z⋅3⁢cos⁡z
    =d⁢z9⁢sin2⁡z
    =19⁢csc2⁡z⁢d⁢z.

    Thus,

    ∫19⁢csc2⁡z⁢d⁢z=−19⁢cot⁡z+C.

    Using the triangle, we get the answer back in terms of x:

    ∫d⁢xx2⁢9−x2=−9−x29⁢x+C.
  10. 10.

    Example 7: Evaluate ∫d⁢x4⁢x2+1.
    Solution: Notice that 4⁢x2+1=(2⁢x)2+1. Let 2⁢x=tan⁡z, so 2⁢d⁢x=sec2⁡z⁢d⁢z, which means d⁢x=12⁢sec2⁡z⁢d⁢z, and z=arctan⁡(2⁢x).

    d⁢x(2⁢x)2+1 =12⁢sec2⁡z⁢d⁢z(tan⁡z)2+1
    =12⁢sec2⁡z⁢d⁢zsec2⁡z
    =12⁢sec2⁡z⁢d⁢zsec⁡z
    =12⁢sec⁡z⁢d⁢z.
    12⁢∫sec⁡z⁢d⁢z=12⁢ln⁡|sec⁡z+tan⁡z|+C.

    Putting things back in terms of x using the triangle,

    ∫d⁢x4⁢x2+1=12⁢ln⁡|4⁢x2+1+2⁢x|+C.

    Example 8: Evaluate ∫x2−3x⁢𝑑x.
    Solution: Let x=3⁢sec⁡z, d⁢x=3⁢sec⁡z⁢tan⁡z⁢d⁢z, x/3=sec⁡z, and z=arcsec⁡(x/3).

    x2−3x⁢d⁢x =3⁢sec⁡z⁢tan⁡z⁢(3⁢sec⁡z)2−3⁢d⁢z3⁢sec⁡z
    =3⁢tan2⁡z⁢d⁢z.

    Integrating,

    ∫3⁢tan2⁡z⁢d⁢z =∫3⁢(sec2⁡z−1)⁢𝑑z
    =3⁢(tan⁡z−z)+C
    =x2−3−3⁢arcsec⁡(x/3)+C.

    Example 9: Evaluate

    ∫01d⁢x1+x2.

    Solution: Since the integrand contains 1+x2, use the substitution

    x=tan⁡θ.

    Then

    d⁢x=sec2⁡θ⁢d⁢θand1+x2=1+tan2⁡θ=sec2⁡θ.

    Rather than drawing a triangle, convert the limits of integration. When x=0, θ=0. When x=1, θ=π/4. Therefore,

    ∫01d⁢x1+x2=∫0π/4sec2⁡θsec2⁡θ⁢𝑑θ=∫0π/41⁢𝑑θ=π4.
