  2. 2.

    Defining a Sequence. We want to investigate the terms of the Taylor polynomial, given by the formula,

    f(n)⁢(0)n!⁢xn.

    In general, we define a sequence to be a function from the positive integers to the real numbers, a⁢(n)=an. The numbers

    a1,a2,…,an

    are called the terms. The number an is called the nth term.

    Example 4: Find a sequence {an} whose first five terms are

    2,43,85,167,329,…

    Solution: We need to find some relationship between the counting digits, 1,2,3,…. Note that the denominators are all odd numbers 1,3,5,…. Odd numbers, are even numbers displaced by 1, and even numbers are all the numbers divisible by 2. Hence we can write the denominator as 2⁢n+1. The numerator consists of powers of 2, and so we write that 2n. Thus, we have that

    an=2n2⁢n+1

  13. 13.

    Next. We are concerned with determining the limits of sequences because we want to learn what its long-term behavior will be. That is, we want to know what the sequence will do as its dependent parameter, n becomes very large.

  14. 14.

    Definition. Let L be a real number. The limit of a sequence {an} is L, written as

    limn→∞an=L

    if for each ϵ>0, there exists a number M>0, such that |an−L|<ϵ whenever n>M. If the limit L, exists, the sequence is said to converge to L. Otherwise, the sequence is said to diverge.

    1. (a)

      This theorem says a few things.

      • •

        The limit of a sequence is L, when an is close to L. We define closeness by saying that their difference is small. As the scientist, you are the one who gets to define closeness, and so you get to choose the value of ϵ to specify at what point you will say the sequence and the limit are identical.

      • •

        Now, you need to know when the sequence will get close to L. This is what is meant by n>M, for values of n that are sufficiently bigger than some index number, the terms are going to be within the good range for us to consider the sequence and the limit identical. This means that eventually we will get to where want, and we know that after a certain value of n, we will be at our destination.

      • •

        The definition is nice for giving us a concept for understanding how we know a limit exists or why it does not exist. However, this is not always helpful for actually finding limits. Note that if we were to graph the sequence for say an=1/n, and compare its shape to the graph of f⁢(x)=1/x, that they are almost identical. The difference is that the domain of the sequence only consists of the natural numbers, whereas the domain of the function consists of all real numbers (except 0). So what we can do is convert the sequence to a function and then calculate the limit. This allows us to use things like L’Hôpital’s Rule.

  15. 15.

    Theorem (Limit of a Sequence) Let L be a real number. Let f be a function of a real variable such that

    limx→∞f⁢(x)=L.

    If {an} is a sequence such that f⁢(n)=an, for every positive integer n, then

    limn→∞an=L.

    (The reverse statement is not true)

    Convergence Properties of Sequences.

    Theorem (Convergence Properties of Sequences).

    Let {an}n=m∞ and {bn}n=m∞ be two convergent sequences, and let c be any real constant. Then

    1. (a)

      limn→∞(an±bn)=limn→∞an±limn→∞bn

    2. (b)

      limn→∞(an⁢bn)=limn→∞an⋅limn→∞bn

    3. (c)

      limn→∞(c⁢an)=c⁢limn→∞an

    4. (d)

      limn→∞(anbn)=limn→∞anlimn→∞bn, provided limn→∞bn≠0.

  21. 21.

    Example 11: Evaluate limn→∞n⁢ln⁡(1+1n).
    Solution: As n→∞, we have n→∞ while

    ln⁡(1+1n)→0.

    Therefore, this limit has the indeterminate form 0⋅∞. Rewrite the product as a quotient:

    n⁢ln⁡(1+1n)=ln⁡(1+1n)1n.

    Since L’Hospital’s Rule applies to functions of a real variable, first consider the associated function

    f⁢(x)=ln⁡(1+1x)1x.

    Then

    limn→∞n⁢ln⁡(1+1n)=limx→∞ln⁡(1+1x)1x,

    provided the limit on the right exists. Now the limit has the indeterminate form 00, so we may apply L’Hospital’s Rule:

    limx→∞ln⁡(1+1x)1x = limx→∞−1x⁢(x+1)−1x2
    = limx→∞xx+1
    = 1.

    Hence,

    limn→∞nln(1+1n)=1.

    Squeezing Theorem

    Theorem (Squeezing Theorem).

    Let limn→∞an=limn→∞bn and if {cn}n=m∞ is any sequence where an≤cn≤bn for n≤m. Then {cn}n=m∞ converges, and

    limn→∞an=limn→∞cn=limn→∞bn
