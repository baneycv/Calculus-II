Math 252, 7.2 Volume by the Disk Method and Washer Method

  1. 1.

    Situation. In this application, we want to determine the volume of various solids. We are going to start with solids of revolution; we take a 2-D graph and rotate it around the x-axis. We will consider the case of revolution around the y-axis separately. The simplest case to consider is the one of the function being a constant function. Supposing f⁢(x)=c on an interval [a,b].

    If we revolve the region bounded under the graph around the x-axis, the result is a disk with a radius R=f⁢(x), and a thickness w=b−a, see figure

    Using the formula for the volume of a cylinder (Vc⁢y⁢l⁢i⁢n⁢d⁢e⁢r=π⁢r2⁢h, r-radius, h-height), we get that the volume of our disc is V=π⁢[f⁢(x)]2⁢(b−a). Notice that the radius is the function, because the distance from the axis of rotation to the edge of the solid is of size f⁢(x), and the thickness, or height of the disc is the size of the interval b−a. We now, apply this to a nonconstant function.

  2. 2.

    Non-Constant Functions. Let f⁢(x) be a continous function on the interval [a,b], and let D by the region bounded between the graph of f, x=a and x=b.

    Suppose, D is revolved around the x-axis, I want to find the volume.

    We proceed as we have previously. Partition the interval [a,b] into n subintervals, where Δ⁢x=b−an. This results in a partition a=x0<x1<x2<⋯<xk<⋯<xn=b, for 1≤k≤n, where xk=a+k⁢Δ⁢x. Considering an arbitrary interval, [xk−1,xk], to define a rectangle in the region, we evaluate the function at some point xk−1≤tk≤xk. So now we have a characteristic rectangle, and similar set up as the simple case above. If we revolve that rectangle around the x−a⁢x⁢i⁢s, this results in a disc with volume Δ⁢V=π⁢[f⁢(tk)]2⁢Δ⁢x. If we do this for all n subintervals, we get

    Vn=∑k=1nπ⁢[f⁢(tk)]2⁢Δ⁢x.

    This is a Riemann Sum, and we know that if n→∞, which means Δ⁢x→0, the sum converges to

    V=∫abπ⁢[f⁢(x)]2⁢𝑑x.

    1. (a)

      Observation 1: The Riemann Sum alerts as to what each of these quantities actually represent. f⁢(x) is the radius of the solid. d⁢x is the thickness of the solid. The integral is telling us to essentially add up a bunch of volumes of cylinders or discs that have a very small thickness.

    2. (b)

      Observation 2: The radius is the distance from the axis of rotation to the edge of the solid. This becomes important if we shift the axis of rotation up or down.

    3. (c)

      Despite what your books says, we will use the Disc method for solids revolved around the x-axis only.

  3. 3.

    Example 1: Let D be the region bounded by the graph of f⁢(x)=sin⁡x on [0,π].

    Determine the volume of the solid formed by revolving R around the x-axis.

    Solution: Since the we are revolving the region around the x-axis, we use the Disc Method. Looking at the graph, the radius will be f⁢(x), and we will integrate between 0 and π. Hence we obtain,

    V = ∫abπ⁢[f⁢(x)]2⁢𝑑x
    = ∫0ππ⁢(sin⁡x)2⁢𝑑x
    = ∫0ππ⁢sin⁡x⁢d⁢x
    = −π⁢cos⁡x|0π
    = 2⁢π
  4. 4.

    Example 2: Let f⁢(x)=2−x2 and g⁢(x)=1. Let D be the region bounded between the graphs of f and g. Determine the volume of the solid formed by revolving D around the line y=1.
    Solution: In this case, we are shifting the axis of rotation. Looking at the 2-D graph, we know the radius of the surface will involve f⁢(x).

    Since we want only the part that is above y=1, the radius will be R⁢(x)=f⁢(x)−1 (How would this change if f were below the axis?). We also need the bounds of integration.

    We solve 2−x2=1 and we get x=1 and x=−1. Hence, we have

    V = ∫abπ⁢[f⁢(x)−1]2⁢𝑑x
    = ∫−11π⁢[(2−x2)−1]⁢𝑑x
    = ∫−11π⁢(1−x2)2⁢𝑑x
    = ∫−11π⁢(1−2⁢x2+x4)⁢𝑑x
    = π⁢(x−23⁢x3+x55)|−11
    = 16⁢π15
  5. 5.

    Washer Method Suppose g⁢(x)≤f⁢(x) for all x in [a,b] and are continuous in the interval.

    Let D be the region bounded between the graphs, we want to find the volume of the solid resulting from revolving D around the x-axis.

    We can certainly derive this formula using a Riemann Sum. However, you can also conceive of it by subtracting the volume of the larger surface of radius f from the surface with the smaller radius g. Hence we get

    V=∫abπ⁢[(f⁢(x))2−(g⁢(x))2]⁢𝑑x.

    We call this the washer method.

  6. 6.

    Example 3: Consider the region D bounded between f⁢(x)=x and g⁢(x)=x2. Determine the volume of the surface formed by revolving D around the x-axis.
    Solution: Since we have two graphs, and we are revolving them around the x-axis, we know that we are going to use the Washer Method (which is really the Disc Method for solids with wholes). We also sketch a picture of D, so we know what we are going to be working with and to find the bounds of integration.

    Solving x=x2, we get that the intersection points are x=0 and x=1. Thus, we have

    V = ∫abπ⁢[(f⁢(x))2−(g⁢(x))2]⁢𝑑x
    = ∫01π⁢[(x)2−(x2)2]⁢𝑑x
    = ∫01π⁢(x−x4)⁢𝑑x
    = 3⁢π10
