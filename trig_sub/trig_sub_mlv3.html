Math 252, 8.4 Trigonometric Substitution

  1. 1.

    Purpose. We now explore integrals that contain square roots of the form

    a2−x2,x2+a2,x2−a2.

    The goal is to eliminate the square root from the problem by using a trigonometric substitution and the corresponding right triangle.

    Trigonometric Substitution (a>0).
    Square root Substitution Triangle
    a2−u2 u=a⁢sin⁡z
    u2+a2 u=a⁢tan⁡z
    u2−a2 u=a⁢sec⁡z

    For these substitutions,

    a2−u2=a⁢cos⁡z,u2+a2=a⁢sec⁡z,u2−a2=a⁢tan⁡z.

    Before using this as an integration technique, we will first become familiar with the algebra associated with simplifying these square roots, then examine how the substitutions produce right triangles, and finally put these ideas together to integrate.

  2. 2.

    Example 1: Suppose x=5⁢sin⁡z. Simplify 25−x2.

    Solution:

    25−x2 =25−(5⁢sin⁡z)2
    =25−25⁢sin2⁡z
    =25⁢(1−sin2⁡z)
    =25⁢cos2⁡z
    =5⁢cos⁡z.

    Example 2: Suppose x=3⁢tan⁡z. Simplify x2+9.

    Solution:

    x2+9 =(3⁢tan⁡z)2+9
    =9⁢tan2⁡z+9
    =9⁢(tan2⁡z+1)
    =9⁢sec2⁡z
    =3⁢sec⁡z.

    Example 3: Suppose x=a⁢sec⁡z. Simplify x2−a2.

    Solution: For x2−a2, let x=a⁢sec⁡z. Then

    x2−a2 =(a⁢sec⁡z)2−a2
    =a2⁢sec2⁡z−a2
    =a2⁢(sec2⁡z−1)
    =a2⁢tan2⁡z
    =a⁢tan⁡z.

    Example 4: Suppose

    sin⁡z=x3.

    Find cos⁡z.

    Solution: Draw a right triangle with opposite side x and hypotenuse 3. By the Pythagorean Theorem, the adjacent side is

    9−x2.

    Therefore,

    cos⁡z=9−x23.

    Example 5: Suppose

    tan⁡z=x4.

    Find sec⁡z.

    Solution: Since tan⁡z=oppositeadjacent, draw a right triangle with opposite side x and adjacent side 4. By the Pythagorean Theorem,

    r2=x2+42=x2+16.

    Therefore,

    r=x2+16.

    Since sec⁡z=hypotenuseadjacent, we obtain

    sec⁡z=x2+164.
  8. 8.

    Integration Technique.

    Integration Technique.
    1. (a)

      Choose the substitution. Identify the square root form and choose the corresponding substitution.

    2. (b)

      Differentiate. Find d⁢x in terms of d⁢z.

    3. (c)

      Draw the triangle. Use the substitution to label the triangle and find the missing side.

    4. (d)

      Substitute and simplify. Replace the square root, x, and d⁢x.

    5. (e)

      Integrate.

    6. (f)

      Back substitute. Use the triangle to rewrite the answer in terms of the original variable.

    Example 6: Evaluate ∫d⁢xx2⁢9−x2.

    Solution: Let x=3⁢sin⁡z. Then d⁢x=3⁢cos⁡z⁢d⁢z and z=arcsin⁡(x/3).

    d⁢xx2⁢9−x2 =3⁢cos⁡z⁢d⁢z(3⁢sin⁡z)2⁢9−(3⁢sin⁡z)2
    =3⁢cos⁡z⁢d⁢z9⁢sin2⁡z⁢9−9⁢sin2⁡z
    =3⁢cos⁡z⁢d⁢z9⁢sin2⁡z⁢9⁢(1−sin2⁡z)
    =3⁢cos⁡z⁢d⁢z9⁢sin2⁡z⁢9⁢cos2⁡z
    =3⁢cos⁡z⁢d⁢z9⁢sin2⁡z⋅3⁢cos⁡z
    =19⁢csc2⁡z⁢d⁢z.

    Thus,

    ∫19⁢csc2⁡z⁢d⁢z=−19⁢cot⁡z+C.

    Using the triangle, cot⁡z=9−x2x. Therefore,

    ∫d⁢xx2⁢9−x2=−9−x29⁢x+C.

    Example 7: Evaluate ∫d⁢x4⁢x2+1.

    Solution: Notice that

    4⁢x2+1=(2⁢x)2+1.

    Let 2⁢x=tan⁡z. Then 2⁢d⁢x=sec2⁡z⁢d⁢z, so d⁢x=12⁢sec2⁡z⁢d⁢z, and z=arctan⁡(2⁢x).

    d⁢x(2⁢x)2+1 =12⁢sec2⁡z⁢d⁢ztan2⁡z+1
    =12⁢sec2⁡z⁢d⁢zsec2⁡z
    =12⁢sec2⁡z⁢d⁢zsec⁡z
    =12⁢sec⁡z⁢d⁢z.

    Therefore,

    12⁢∫sec⁡z⁢d⁢z=12⁢ln⁡|sec⁡z+tan⁡z|+C.

    Using the triangle, sec⁡z=4⁢x2+1 and tan⁡z=2⁢x. Hence,

    ∫d⁢x4⁢x2+1=12⁢ln⁡|4⁢x2+1+2⁢x|+C.

    Example 8: Evaluate ∫x2−3x⁢𝑑x.

    Solution: Let x=3⁢sec⁡z. Then d⁢x=3⁢sec⁡z⁢tan⁡z⁢d⁢z and z=arcsec⁡(x/3).

    x2−3x⁢d⁢x =(3⁢sec⁡z)2−33⁢sec⁡z⋅3⁢sec⁡z⁢tan⁡z⁢d⁢z
    =3⁢tan2⁡z⁢d⁢z.

    Integrating,

    ∫3⁢tan2⁡z⁢d⁢z =∫3⁢(sec2⁡z−1)⁢𝑑z
    =3⁢(tan⁡z−z)+C.

    Using the triangle, tan⁡z=x2−33 and z=arcsec⁡(x/3). Therefore,

    ∫x2−3x⁢𝑑x=x2−3−3⁢arcsec⁡(x3)+C.

    Example 9: Evaluate

    ∫01d⁢x1+x2.

    Solution: Since the integrand contains 1+x2, use the substitution

    x=tan⁡θ.

    Then

    d⁢x=sec2⁡θ⁢d⁢θ

    and

    1+x2=1+tan2⁡θ=sec2⁡θ.

    Rather than drawing a triangle, convert the limits of integration.

    When x=0,

    tan⁡θ=0⟹θ=0.

    When x=1,

    tan⁡θ=1⟹θ=π4.

    Therefore,

    ∫01d⁢x1+x2 =∫0π/4sec2⁡θsec2⁡θ⁢𝑑θ
    =∫0π/41⁢𝑑θ
    =θ|0π/4
    =π4.
