Math 252, 9.9 & 9.10, Taylor Series, Part 2 (Using the Geometric Series)

  1. 1.

    Recall. Last time we established Taylor’s Theorem, and looked specifically at how Taylor Series (for which the partial sums are Taylor Polynomials) are Power Series. This meant that we could use what we learned about Power Series (specifically for those that were derived from the sum of a Geometric Series). In order to review, let f be a continuously differentiable function for an interval I centered at x=0. The n-th degree Taylor polynomial, centered around x=0, was defined to be,

    pn⁢(x)=f⁢(0)+f′⁢(0)⁢x+f′′⁢(0)2!⁢x2+f′′′⁢(0)3!⁢x3+⋯+f(n)⁢(0)n!⁢xn.

    Using the graph of the polynomial, we observed as n→∞ that pn⁢(x)→f⁢(x).

    We have that pn⁢(x) would represent the n-th partial sum of the series. From what we have learned thus far, this means that there will be a gap between the actual sum, and the truncated sum. We called this the remainder. For polynomials, this will be another function, Rn⁢(x) to be

    Rn⁢(x)=f⁢(x)−pn⁢(x).

    This leads us to the following theorem which will give us a formula for Rn⁢(x). We will offer without proof.

    Theorem 1

    (Taylor’s Theorem) If a function f has derivatives of all orders at x=0, then for

    pn=f⁢(0)+f′⁢(0)⁢x+⋯+f(n)⁢(0)n!⁢xn

    and

    Rn⁢(x)=f(n+1)⁢(z)(n+1)!⁢xn+1

    it follows that f⁢(x)=pn⁢(x)+Rn⁢(x). Furthermore, as n→∞, pn⁢(x)→f⁢(x), and Rn⁢(x)→0 for all x within the interval of convergence. Thus,

    f⁢(x)=∑n=0∞f(n)⁢(0)n!⁢xn

    This theorem gives us two conclusions:

    • •

      Firstly, continuously differentiable (analytic) functions can be expressed as Taylor series.

    • •

      We have equality between the two so long as the series is convergent and the remainder goes to zero.

    We therefore, can define what is called Taylor Series for a function which is a power series as well. It also gives us tools for determining how close the n-th partial sum (or truncated polynomial) is to the exact function.

  2. 2.

    In this lecture… we will focus on three functions, f⁢(x)=ex, g⁢(x)=sin⁡x, and h⁢(x)=cos⁡x in order to explore the Remainder and establish whether or not the Taylor Series converges to the functions. In order to show this, we will need to show Rn⁢(x)→0 for all x within the interval of convergence. The primary method for doing this will be:

    • •

      Write an expression for Rn⁢(x), keeping in mind that we need to write some expression for f(n+1)⁢(z) where z is some value between 0 and x. We treat z as a variable as we do not know what it actually is.

    • •

      We then find an upper bound for |Rn⁢(x)| which should be in terms of only n.

    • •

      Fix the value of x (we assume this value does not change). We then show that the upper bound converges to 0, which would enable us to apply the Squeezing Theorem to conclude that Rn⁢(x) also converges to 0 for all values of x in the interval.

  3. 3.

    Example 1: Show that

    ex=∑n=0∞xnn!

    for any value of x.

    Solution: We first establish the radius of convergence for the series. Using the Ratio Test, we see that

    limn→∞|xn+1(n+1)!⋅n!xn|=limn→∞|x|n+1=0.

    Since 0<1 for every real number x, the series converges for all x∈ℝ. Thus, its radius of convergence is R=∞.

    We now show that the series converges specifically to ex. The Taylor polynomial of degree n for f⁢(x)=ex centered at x=0 is

    Pn⁢(x)=∑k=0nxkk!.

    By Taylor’s Theorem,

    ex=Pn⁢(x)+Rn⁢(x),

    where the remainder has the form

    Rn⁢(x)=ec(n+1)!⁢xn+1

    for some number c between 0 and x.

    For a fixed value of x, we have

    ec≤e|x|,

    and therefore

    |Rn⁢(x)|≤e|x|⁢|x|n+1(n+1)!.

    Now consider the sequence

    an=e|x|⁢|x|n+1(n+1)!.

    Its consecutive-term ratio is

    an+1an=|x|n+2,

    which approaches 0 as n→∞. Hence,

    limn→∞an=0.

    It follows from the Squeeze Theorem that

    limn→∞Rn⁢(x)=0.

    Therefore,

    ex=limn→∞Pn⁢(x)=∑n=0∞xnn!

    for every x∈ℝ.

    Example 2: Show that

    sin⁡x=∑n=0∞(−1)n⁢x2⁢n+1(2⁢n+1)!

    for all values of x.

    Solution: We first establish the radius of convergence for the series. Using the Ratio Test, we see that

    limn→∞|(−1)n+1⁢x2⁢n+3(2⁢n+3)!(−1)n⁢x2⁢n+1(2⁢n+1)!| =limn→∞|x|2⁢n+3(2⁢n+3)!⋅(2⁢n+1)!|x|2⁢n+1
    =limn→∞x2(2⁢n+2)⁢(2⁢n+3)
    =0.

    Since 0<1 for every real number x, the series converges for all x∈ℝ. Thus, its radius of convergence is R=∞.

    We now show that the series converges specifically to sin⁡x. The derivatives of f⁢(x)=sin⁡x repeat in a cycle:

    kf(k)⁢(x)0sin⁡x1cos⁡x2−sin⁡x3−cos⁡x4sin⁡x

    At x=0, these derivatives have the values

    f⁢(0)=0,f′⁢(0)=1,f′′⁢(0)=0,f(3)⁢(0)=−1,

    and the pattern repeats every four derivatives. Therefore,

    f(2⁢n)⁢(0)=0

    and

    f(2⁢n+1)⁢(0)=(−1)n.

    It follows that the Maclaurin polynomial of degree 2⁢n+1 is

    P2⁢n+1⁢(x)=∑k=0n(−1)k⁢x2⁢k+1(2⁢k+1)!.

    By Taylor’s Theorem,

    sin⁡x=P2⁢n+1⁢(x)+R2⁢n+1⁢(x),

    where

    R2⁢n+1⁢(x)=f(2⁢n+2)⁢(c)(2⁢n+2)!⁢x2⁢n+2

    for some number c between 0 and x.

    Since every derivative of sin⁡x is one of

    sin⁡x,cos⁡x,−sin⁡x,−cos⁡x,

    we have

    |f(2⁢n+2)⁢(c)|≤1.

    Therefore,

    |R2⁢n+1⁢(x)|≤|x|2⁢n+2(2⁢n+2)!.

    For each fixed value of x,

    limn→∞|x|2⁢n+2(2⁢n+2)!=0.

    Thus, by the Squeeze Theorem,

    limn→∞R2⁢n+1⁢(x)=0.

    Therefore,

    sin⁡x=limn→∞P2⁢n+1⁢(x)=∑n=0∞(−1)n⁢x2⁢n+1(2⁢n+1)!

    for every x∈ℝ.

    Example 3 Let f⁢(x)=cos⁡(x2).

    1. (a)

      Using the Taylor Series cos⁡x=∑n=0∞(−1)n⁢x2⁢n(2⁢n)!, determine a Taylor Series around x=0 for f⁢(x).

    2. (b)

      Using part (a), evaluate

      ∫01f⁢(t)⁢𝑑t

      to within .001 of the actual value.

    Solution:

    1. (a)

      Substituting x2 for x in the Taylor series for cos⁡x, we obtain

      cos⁡(x2)=∑n=0∞(−1)n⁢(x2)2⁢n(2⁢n)!.

      Since

      (x2)2⁢n=x4⁢n,

      it follows that

      cos⁡(x2)=∑n=0∞(−1)n⁢x4⁢n(2⁢n)!.

      Writing out the first several terms gives

      cos⁡(x2)=1−x42!+x84!−x126!+x168!−⋯.
    2. (b)

      Using part (a), evaluate

      ∫01f⁢(t)⁢𝑑t

      to within .001 of the actual value.

      Solution: From part (a),

      cos⁡(t2)=∑n=0∞(−1)n⁢t4⁢n(2⁢n)!.

      Integrating term by term from 0 to 1, we obtain

      ∫01cos⁡(t2)⁢𝑑t =∫01∑n=0∞(−1)n⁢t4⁢n(2⁢n)!⁢d⁢t
      =∑n=0∞(−1)n(2⁢n)!⁢∫01t4⁢n⁢𝑑t
      =∑n=0∞(−1)n⁢1(4⁢n+1)⁢(2⁢n)!.

      Thus,

      ∫01cos⁡(t2)⁢𝑑t=1−15⁢(2!)+19⁢(4!)−113⁢(6!)+⋯.

      We first determine how many terms are needed. Since this is an alternating series whose terms decrease in magnitude, the Alternating Series Estimation Theorem gives

      |RN|≤1(4⁢N+5)⁢(2⁢N+2)!,

      where RN is the error obtained by using the terms corresponding to n=0,1,…,N.

      We require

      1(4⁢N+5)⁢(2⁢N+2)!<0.001.

      Testing N=1, we obtain

      1(4⁢(1)+5)⁢(2⁢(1)+2)!=19⁢(4!)=1216≈0.00463,

      which is not less than 0.001.

      Testing N=2, we obtain

      1(4⁢(2)+5)⁢(2⁢(2)+2)!=113⁢(6!)=19360≈0.000107,

      which is less than 0.001.

      Therefore, we must use the terms corresponding to

      n=0,n=1,andn=2.

      That is, three terms are required. Hence,

      ∫01cos⁡(t2)⁢𝑑t ≈1−15⁢(2!)+19⁢(4!)
      =1−110+1216
      ≈0.904630.

      Therefore,

      ∫01cos⁡(t2)⁢𝑑t≈0.905.

    Example 4 (Putting Chapter 9 Together): Let

    f⁢(x)=x⁢ln⁡(1+2⁢x).
    1. (a)

      Using the Taylor series

      ln⁡(1+x)=∑n=1∞(−1)n+1⁢xnn,

    2. (b)

      Determine the interval of convergence of the Taylor series.

    3. (c)

      Using part (a), express

      ∫01/2x⁢ln⁡(1+2⁢x)⁢𝑑x

      as an infinite series.

    4. (d)

      Determine the value of the integral to within .01 of the actual value.

    Solution:

    1. (a)

      Beginning with

      ln⁡(1+x)=∑n=1∞(−1)n+1⁢xnn,

      we substitute 2⁢x for x to obtain

      ln⁡(1+2⁢x)=∑n=1∞(−1)n+1⁢(2⁢x)nn.

      Since

      (2⁢x)n=2n⁢xn,

      it follows that

      ln⁡(1+2⁢x)=∑n=1∞(−1)n+1⁢2n⁢xnn.

      Multiplying both sides by x gives

      xln(1+2x)=∑n=1∞(−1)n+12n⁢xn+1n.

      Writing out the first several terms,

      x⁢ln⁡(1+2⁢x)=2⁢x2−2⁢x3+83⁢x4−4⁢x5+325⁢x6−⋯.
    2. (b)

      We use the Ratio Test. Let

      an=(−1)n+1⁢2n⁢xn+1n.

      Then

      limn→∞|an+1an| =limn→∞|(−1)n+2⁢2n+1⁢xn+2n+1⋅n(−1)n+1⁢2n⁢xn+1|
      =limn→∞2⁢|x|⁢nn+1
      =2⁢|x|.

      The Ratio Test gives convergence whenever

      2⁢|x|<1,

      or

      |x|<12.

      Thus the radius of convergence is

      R=12.

      We now test the endpoints.

      When

      x=12,

      the series becomes

      ∑n=1∞(−1)n+1⁢1n,

      which converges by the Alternating Series Test.

      When

      x=−12,

      the series becomes

      −∑n=1∞1n,

      which diverges.

      Therefore, the interval of convergence is

      (−12,12].
    3. (c)

      Since

      [0,12]

      lies within the interval of convergence, we may integrate the series term-by-term:

      ∫01/2x⁢ln⁡(1+2⁢x)⁢𝑑x =∫01/2∑n=1∞(−1)n+1⁢2n⁢xn+1n⁢d⁢x
      =∑n=1∞(−1)n+1⁢2nn⁢∫01/2xn+1⁢𝑑x
      =∑n=1∞(−1)n+1⁢2nn⁢[xn+2n+2]01/2.

      2n⁢(12)n+2=14,

      ∫01/2xln(1+2x)dx=14∑n=1∞(−1)n+1n⁢(n+2).

      Writing out the first several terms,

      ∫01/2x⁢ln⁡(1+2⁢x)⁢𝑑x=112−132+160−196+1140−⋯.
    4. (d)

      The series

      14⁢∑n=1∞(−1)n+1n⁢(n+2)

      14⁢∑n=1∞|(−1)n+1n⁢(n+2)|=14⁢∑n=1∞1n⁢(n+2)

      which is a telescoping series that converges absolutely (you can show this on your own). Therefore, we know that our integral yields a finite sum. Therefore, the Alternating Series Estimation Theorem applies.

      If the terms corresponding to

      n=1,2,…,N

      are used, then

      |RN|≤14⁢(N+1)⁢(N+3).

      We require

      14⁢(N+1)⁢(N+3)<0.01.

      so three terms are not sufficient.
