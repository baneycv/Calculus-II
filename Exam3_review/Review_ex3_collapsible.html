  1. 1.

    Let f⁢(x)=ln⁡(1+2⁢x).

    1. (a)

      Using derivatives, determine the fourth degree Taylor polynomial around x=0 for f.

      Show Solution

      Solution: We compute the derivatives:

      f⁢(x) =ln⁡(1+2⁢x), f⁢(0) =0,
      f′⁢(x) =21+2⁢x, f′⁢(0) =2,
      f′′⁢(x) =−4(1+2⁢x)2, f′′⁢(0) =−4,
      f′′′⁢(x) =16(1+2⁢x)3, f′′′⁢(0) =16,
      f(4)⁢(x) =−96(1+2⁢x)4, f(4)⁢(0) =−96.

      Therefore,

      P4⁢(x) =f⁢(0)+f′⁢(0)⁢x+f′′⁢(0)2!⁢x2+f′′′⁢(0)3!⁢x3+f(4)⁢(0)4!⁢x4
      =2⁢x−2⁢x2+83⁢x3−4⁢x4.

      Thus,

      P4⁢(x)=2⁢x−2⁢x2+83⁢x3−4⁢x4.
    2. (b)

      Using part (a), determine the general term, and use it to evaluate f(10)⁢(0).

      Show Solution

      Solution: The pattern in part (a) gives

      ln⁡(1+2⁢x)=∑n=1∞(−1)n+1⁢2n⁢xnn.

      The coefficient of x10 is

      (−1)11⁢21010=−21010.

      Since the coefficient of x10 in a Taylor series is f(10)⁢(0)/10!,

      f(10)⁢(0)10!=−21010.

      Hence,

      f(10)⁢(0)=−210⁢ 9!.

      Using derivatives, determine the third degree Taylor polynomial around x=0 for f.

      Show Solution

      Solution: We compute the derivatives needed at x=0:

      f⁢(0)=1.

      Also,

      f′⁢(x)=−2⁢x⁢e−x2,f′⁢(0)=0.

      Differentiating again,

      f′′⁢(x)=(−2+4⁢x2)⁢e−x2,f′′⁢(0)=−2.

      Since f is an even function, every odd derivative at 0 is zero. Thus,

      f′′′⁢(0)=0.

      Therefore,

      P3⁢(x) =f⁢(0)+f′⁢(0)⁢x+f′′⁢(0)2!⁢x2+f′′′⁢(0)3!⁢x3
      =1−x2.

      Thus,

      P3⁢(x)=1−x2.
    2. (b)

      Using part (a), determine the general term, and use it to evaluate f(7)⁢(0).

      Show Solution

      Solution: The pattern is

      e−x2=∑n=0∞(−1)n⁢x2⁢nn!.

      Only even powers of x occur, so the coefficient of x7 is zero. Therefore,

      f(7)⁢(0)7!=0,

      and hence

      f(7)⁢(0)=0.

  4. 4.

    Determine whether each series converges or diverges. Give reasons.

    1. (a)

      ∑n=1∞2⁢nn3+1.

      Show Solution

      Solution: We observe that for large n

      2⁢nn3+1∼2⁢nn3=2n2.

      This would mean that for large values of n, our series resembles a p-series,

      ∑n=1∞2n2,

      for which p=2, which means it is convergent. Therefore, we use Limit Comparison with ∑2/n2:

      limn→∞2⁢nn3+12n2=limn→∞2⁢n32⁢(n3+1)=1.

      Since 0<2<∞ and ∑1/n2 converges, the given series converges.

      Converges by the Limit Comparison Test.
    2. (b)

      ∑n=1∞n2en.

      Show Solution

      Solution: This series has some similarity to a Geometric Series. However, due to the n2, we can’t treat it, or compare it to one. Therefore, on account of the power of n in the denominator, we use the Ratio Test. Let an=n2/en. Then

      L =limn→∞|an+1an|
      =limn→∞(n+1)2en+1⁢enn2
      =1e⁢limn→∞(n+1n)2=1e<1.

      Therefore,

      the series converges by the Ratio Test.
    3. (c)

      ∑n=1∞3⁢n2+12⁢n2+4⁢n+1.

      Show Solution

      Solution: We observe that for large values of n,

      3⁢n2+12⁢n2+4⁢n+1∼3⁢n22⁢n2=32≠0.

      Apply the Divergence Test:

      limn→∞3⁢n2+12⁢n2+4⁢n+1=32≠0.

      Therefore,

      the series diverges by the Divergence Test.
    4. (d)

      ∑n=2∞1n⁢(ln⁡n)2.

      Show Solution

      Solution: This is not a known series, nor does it resemble a known series. Furthermore, we see that 1n⁢(ln⁡n)2→0 as n→∞, which does not tell us much. We do observe that for the functions f⁢(x)=(ln⁡x)−2 and g⁢(x)=1x, we do have a derivative relationship. This tells us we should use the Integral Test with

      f⁢(x)=1x⁢(ln⁡x)2.

      For x≥2, f is positive, continuous, and decreasing. Moreover,

      ∫2∞d⁢xx⁢(ln⁡x)2 =limb→∞∫2bd⁢xx⁢(ln⁡x)2.

      Let u=ln⁡x, so d⁢u=d⁢x/x. Then

      ∫2∞d⁢xx⁢(ln⁡x)2 =limb→∞∫ln⁡2ln⁡bu−2⁢𝑑u
      =limb→∞[−1u]ln⁡2ln⁡b
      =1ln⁡2.

      The improper integral converges, so

      the series converges by the Integral Test.
    5. (e)

      ∑n=2∞2⁢n3−1n3−1.

      Show Solution

      Solution: We note that for large values of n,

      2⁢n3−1n3−1∼2⁢n3n3=2≠0

      Thus, applying the Divergence test, we find that,

      limn→∞2⁢n3−1n3−1=2≠0.

      Thus, even with the corrected lower index,

      the series diverges by the Divergence Test.
  5. 5.

    Consider

    ∑n=1∞(−1)n⁢1n+5.

    Determine whether it converges absolutely, converges conditionally, or diverges.

    Show Solution

    Solution: Let

    bn=1n+5.

    The sequence bn is decreasing and

    limn→∞bn=0.

    Therefore, the original series converges by the Alternating Series Test.

    For absolute convergence, consider

    ∑n=1∞|(−1)n⁢1n+5|=∑n=1∞1n+5.

    This has the same behavior as the divergent p-series ∑1/n, where p=1/2. Hence the series does not converge absolutely.

    Therefore,

    the series converges conditionally.
  6. 6.

    Consider

    ∑n=1∞(−1)n⁢nn+3.

    Determine whether it converges absolutely, converges conditionally, or diverges.

    Show Solution

    Solution: Let

    bn=nn+3.

    First,

    limn→∞nn+3=0.

    Also, for x>0, let

    g⁢(x)=xx+3.

    Then

    g′⁢(x)=3−x2⁢x⁢(x+3)2.

    Thus g′⁢(x)<0 for x>3, so bn is eventually decreasing. The alternating series therefore converges by the Alternating Series Test.

    For absolute convergence, compare with ∑1/n:

    limn→∞nn+31n=limn→∞nn+3=1.

    Since ∑1/n diverges, the absolute-value series diverges by Limit Comparison.

    Therefore,

    the series converges conditionally.

    Let

    f⁢(x)=∑n=1∞(−1)n⁢3n⁢x2⁢n+1.
    1. (a)

      Determine the radius of convergence, R, of f.

      Show Solution

      Solution: Let

      an=(−1)n⁢3n⁢x2⁢n+1.

      Using the Ratio Test,

      L =limn→∞|an+1an|
      =limn→∞|(−1)n+1⁢3n+1⁢x2⁢(n+1)+1(−1)n⁢3n⁢x2⁢n+1|
      =limn→∞|(−1)n+1(−1)n⋅3n+13n⋅x2⁢(n+1)+1x2⁢n+1|
      =limn→∞|(−1)n+1−n⋅3n+1−n⋅x 2⁢(n+1)+1−(2⁢n+1)|
      =limn→∞|(−1)1⋅31⋅x 2⁢n+2+1−2⁢n−1|
      =limn→∞|−3⁢x2|
      =3⁢|x|2
      =3⁢x2.

      By the Ratio Test, the series converges when

      L<1.

      Therefore,

      3⁢x2 <1,
      x2 <13,
      |x| <13,
      |x| <13.

      Thus, the series converges for

      |x−0|<13.

      Therefore, the radius of convergence is

      R=13=33.
    2. (b)

      Use the power series to approximate

      ∫01/3f⁢(t)⁢𝑑t

      to within .01 of the exact value.

      Show Solution

      Solution: Integrating the power series term by term,

      ∫01/3f⁢(t)⁢𝑑t =∫01/3∑n=1∞(−1)n⁢3n⁢t2⁢n+1⁢d⁢t
      =∑n=1∞(−1)n⁢3n⁢∫01/3t2⁢n+1⁢𝑑t

      =∑n=1∞(−1)n⁢3n(2⁢n+2)⁢32⁢n+2
      =∑n=1∞(−1)n(2⁢n+2)⁢3n+2.

      The Alternating Series Error Bound applies.

      We need to solve

      bN+1<.01.

      Since

      bN+1 =1(2⁢(N+1)+2)⁢3(N+1)+2
      =1(2⁢N+4)⁢3N+3,

      we need

      1(2⁢N+4)⁢3N+3<.01.

      =16⋅34
