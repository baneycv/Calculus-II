Math 252, 9.3 & 9.4: Tests for Positive Series
(Integral Test and Comparison Tests)

  1. (1)

    The Integral Test

    Theorem: Integral Test.

    If f is positive, continuous, and decreasing for x≥1 and an=f⁢(n), then

    ∑n=1∞anand∫1∞f⁢(x)⁢𝑑x

    either both converge or both diverge.

    Proof. Let f be positive, continuous, and decreasing on [1,∞), with f⁢(n)=an. Partition the interval [1,n+1] into subintervals of length 1.

    Since f is decreasing, on the interval [n,n+1] we have

    an+1≤f⁢(x)≤an.

    Therefore,

    ∫nn+1an+1⁢𝑑x≤∫nn+1f⁢(x)⁢𝑑x≤∫nn+1an⁢𝑑x,

    and hence

    an+1≤∫nn+1f⁢(x)⁢𝑑x≤an.

    Adding these inequalities gives

    0<a2+a3+⋯+an+1 =∫12a2⁢𝑑x+∫23a3⁢𝑑x+⋯+∫nn+1an+1⁢𝑑x
    ≤∫12f⁢(x)⁢𝑑x+∫23f⁢(x)⁢𝑑x+⋯+∫nn+1f⁢(x)⁢𝑑x
    =∫1n+1f⁢(x)⁢𝑑x.

    Thus, if the improper integral converges, then the corresponding sequence of partial sums is bounded above and increasing, so the series converges. A similar comparison with the upper rectangles shows that if the integral diverges, then the series also diverges.

    1. (a)

      How do we use the Integral Test? First check that the terms are positive and eventually decreasing. Then define a function f with f⁢(n)=an and evaluate the improper integral. If the integral converges, the series converges. If the integral diverges, the series diverges. If the series is decreasing only after some integer N, we may apply the test starting at N; changing finitely many terms does not affect convergence.

    2. (b)

      The p-Series Test

      Theorem: p-Series Test.

      The series

      ∑n=1∞1np

      converges if and only if p>1.

      Proof. For p>1, let

      f⁢(x)=1xp.

      Then f is positive, continuous, and decreasing on [1,∞). By the Integral Test,

      ∫1∞1xp⁢𝑑x =limb→∞∫1bx−p⁢𝑑x
      =limb→∞[x1−p1−p]1b
      =limb→∞b1−p−11−p
      =1p−1.

      So the integral converges when p>1, and therefore the p-series converges when p>1.

      If p=1, then

      ∫1∞1x⁢𝑑x=limb→∞ln⁡b=∞.

      If p<1, then 1−p>0, so b1−p→∞, and the integral also diverges. Hence the series diverges for p≤1.

      Example 1. Determine whether ∑n=1∞1n2 converges or diverges.

      Solution.

      This is a p-series with p=2. Since 2>1, the series converges.

      The series converges.

      Example 2. Determine whether ∑n=1∞1n converges or diverges.

      Solution.

      This is the harmonic series. It is a p-series with p=1. Since p≤1, the series diverges.

      The series diverges.
    3. (c)

      A logarithmic example

      Example 3. Determine whether ∑n=2∞1n⁢ln⁡n converges or diverges.

      Solution.

      Let

      f⁢(x)=1x⁢ln⁡x,x≥2.

      This function is positive and decreasing on [2,∞), so we may apply the Integral Test. Using the substitution u=ln⁡x, d⁢u=1x⁢d⁢x, we get

      ∫2∞1x⁢ln⁡x⁢𝑑x =limb→∞∫2b1x⁢ln⁡x⁢𝑑x
      =limb→∞[ln⁡(ln⁡x)]2b
      =limb→∞(ln⁡(ln⁡b)−ln⁡(ln⁡2))
      =∞.

      Therefore, by the Integral Test, the series diverges.

      The series diverges.
  2. (2)

    The Comparison Test

    Theorem: Comparison Test.

    Suppose 0<an≤bn for all sufficiently large n.

    1. (a)

      If ∑n=1∞bn converges, then ∑n=1∞an converges.

    2. (b)

      If ∑n=1∞an diverges, then ∑n=1∞bn diverges.

    1. (a)

      Think of the test this way: if a positive series is bounded above by a convergent series, then it cannot grow without bound. On the other hand, if a smaller positive series diverges, then any larger positive series must also diverge.

    2. (b)

      Known series for comparison

      Common Comparison Series.

      Common choices include:

      • •

        telescoping series,

      • •

        geometric series, which converge when |r|<1 and diverge when |r|≥1,

      • •

        p-series, which converge when p>1 and diverge when p≤1,

      • •

        any series whose convergence can be determined by the Integral Test.

    3. (c)

      How do we choose the comparison series? Compare the dominant, or leading, behavior of the terms. For example,

      1n2+2⁢n−3∼1n2,

      so the relevant comparison is a p-series with p=2. Similarly,

      1n3+3n∼13n=3−n,

      because exponential growth eventually dominates polynomial growth. This suggests a convergent geometric comparison.

      Example 4. Determine whether ∑n=1∞12n+1 converges or diverges.

      Solution.

      For large n,

      12n+1∼12n.

      Since

      0<12n+1≤12n,

      and ∑12n is a convergent geometric series with ratio r=12, the Comparison Test shows that the given series converges.

      The series converges.

      Example 5. Determine whether ∑n=1∞cos2⁡nn3/2 converges or diverges.

      Solution.

      Since 0≤cos2⁡n≤1, we have

      0≤cos2⁡nn3/2≤1n3/2.

      The series ∑1n3/2 is a convergent p-series because p=32>1. Therefore, by the Comparison Test, the given series converges.

      The series converges.

      Example 6. Determine whether ∑n=1∞1n+n converges or diverges.

      Solution.

      For large n,

      1n+n∼1n,

      so we expect harmonic behavior. Since n>n for n>1, we have

      n+n<2⁢n.

      Taking reciprocals gives

      12⁢n<1n+n.

      The series ∑12⁢n=12⁢∑1n diverges. Since the given series is larger than a divergent positive series, the Comparison Test shows that it diverges.

      The series diverges.
  3. (3)

    The Limit Comparison Test

    Theorem: Limit Comparison Test.

    Let ∑n=1∞an and ∑n=1∞bn be positive series. Suppose

    limn→∞anbn=L,

    where 0<L<∞. Then both series have the same behavior: either both converge or both diverge.

    1. (a)

      Use the Limit Comparison Test when the ordinary Comparison Test is awkward but the leading behavior of the terms is clear.

    2. (b)

      Choose bn using the same dominant-term reasoning used for the Comparison Test.

      Example 7. Determine whether ∑n=1∞4⁢n−3n3−5⁢n−7 converges or diverges.

      Solution.

      For large n,

      4⁢n−3n3−5⁢n−7∼4⁢nn3=4n2.

      We compare with

      bn=4n2,

      which gives a convergent p-series. Then

      limn→∞4⁢n−3n3−5⁢n−74n2 =limn→∞n2⁢(4⁢n−3)4⁢(n3−5⁢n−7)
      =1.

      Since the limit is positive and finite, both series have the same behavior. Therefore, the given series converges.

      The series converges.

      Example 8. Determine whether ∑n=1∞18⁢n2−5⁢n3 converges or diverges.

      Solution.

      For large n,

      18⁢n2−5⁢n3∼18⁢n23=12⁢n2/3.

      We compare with

      bn=12⁢n2/3,

      which is a divergent p-series because p=23<1. Now compute

      limn→∞18⁢n2−5⁢n312⁢n2/3 =limn→∞2⁢n2/3(8⁢n2−5⁢n)1/3
      =limn→∞2⁢n2/3n2/3⁢(8−5n)1/3
      =283=1.

      Since the limit is positive and finite, both series have the same behavior. Therefore, the given series diverges.

      The series diverges.
