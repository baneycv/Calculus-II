Math 252, 9.2, Series and Convergence

  1. 1.

    What needs to be proven first. Before we can use an infinite sum, we need to answer two questions.

    1. (a)

      Does the infinite sum have a finite value?

    2. (b)

      If it does, what is that value?

  2. 2.

    Definition of an Infinite Series

    Given an infinite sequence {an}, an infinite series is the sum

    ∑n=0∞an=a0+a1+a2+⋯+an+⋯.

    The numbers a0,a1,a2,… are called the terms of the series.

    1. (a)

      The nth partial sum is

      Sn=a0+a1+a2+⋯+an.
    2. (b)

      If

      limn→∞Sn=S,

      and S is a finite number, then the series is said to converge. In that case, the sum of the series is S.

    3. (c)

      If the sequence of partial sums does not converge to a finite number, then the series is said to diverge.

  3. 3.

    Example 1: Determine whether the series ∑n=1∞1 converges or diverges.

    Solution. We find the sequence of partial sums:

    S1=1,S2=1+1=2,S3=1+1+1=3.

    In general,

    Sn=n.

    Therefore,

    limn→∞Sn=limn→∞n=∞.

    Hence the series diverges.

  4. 4.

    Example 2: Determine whether ∑n=1∞1n converges or diverges.

    Solution. This is the harmonic series:

    ∑n=1∞1n=1+12+13+14+15+⋯.

    Group the terms as follows:

    1+12+(13+14)+(15+16+17+18)+⋯.

    Each grouped block after the first two terms is at least 12:

    13+14>14+14=12,

    and

    15+16+17+18>4⁢(18)=12.

    Continuing in this way, the partial sums eventually exceed any fixed number. Therefore the harmonic series diverges.

  5. 5.

    Example 3: Determine whether the following series converges or diverges:

    ∑n=0∞1n+1.

    Solution. Write out the first few terms:

    ∑n=0∞1n+1=1+12+13+14+⋯.

    This is the harmonic series, only written with the index starting at n=0 instead of n=1. Therefore the series diverges.

  6. 6.

    Telescoping Series. Determine whether the following series converges or diverges:

    ∑n=1∞1n⁢(n+1).

    Solution. Using partial fractions,

    1n⁢(n+1)=1n−1n+1.

    Thus

    ∑n=1∞1n⁢(n+1)=∑n=1∞(1n−1n+1).

    The nth partial sum is

    Sn =(1−12)+(12−13)+(13−14)+⋯+(1n−1n+1)
    =1−1n+1.

    Therefore,

    limn→∞Sn=limn→∞(1−1n+1)=1.

    So the series converges and has sum 1.

  7. 7.

    Example 4: Find the sum of the series

    ∑n=1∞24⁢n2−1.

    Solution. First factor the denominator:

    4⁢n2−1=(2⁢n−1)⁢(2⁢n+1).

    Using partial fractions,

    24⁢n2−1=12⁢n−1−12⁢n+1.

    Therefore,

    Sn =(1−13)+(13−15)+(15−17)+⋯+(12⁢n−1−12⁢n+1)
    =1−12⁢n+1.

    Thus

    limn→∞Sn=limn→∞(1−12⁢n+1)=1.

    The series converges and has sum 1.

  8. 8.

    Definition of a Geometric Series

    The series

    ∑n=0∞a⁢rn=a+a⁢r+a⁢r2+a⁢r3+⋯

    is called a geometric series. The number a is the first term, and r is the common ratio.

    The nth partial sum is

    Sn=a+a⁢r+a⁢r2+⋯+a⁢rn.

    Multiplying both sides by r gives

    r⁢Sn=a⁢r+a⁢r2+a⁢r3+⋯+a⁢rn+1.

    Subtract:

    Sn−r⁢Sn =a−a⁢rn+1,
    Sn⁢(1−r) =a⁢(1−rn+1),
    Sn =a⁢(1−rn+1)1−r,r≠1.

    To determine whether the infinite series converges, we take the limit of the partial sums:

    ∑n=0∞a⁢rn=limn→∞Sn.

    If |r|<1, then rn+1→0. Therefore,

    limn→∞Sn=limn→∞a⁢(1−rn+1)1−r=a⁢(1−0)1−r=a1−r.

    Thus, the geometric series converges when |r|<1.

    If |r|≥1 and a≠0, then the terms a⁢rn do not approach 0. Therefore, the series cannot converge. This gives the following test.

    Geometric Series Test

    The geometric series

    ∑n=0∞a⁢rn

    converges if |r|<1. In that case,

    ∑n=0∞a⁢rn=a1−r.

    If |r|≥1 and a≠0, the series diverges.

  9. 9.

    Example 5: Determine whether the series converges or diverges:

    ∑n=0∞32n.

    Solution. Rewrite the series as

    ∑n=0∞3⁢(12)n.

    This is geometric with

    a=3,r=12.

    Since |r|<1, the series converges. Its sum is

    ∑n=0∞32n=31−12=6.
  10. 10.

    Example 6: Determine whether the series converges or diverges:

    ∑n=0∞3n+122⁢n.

    Solution. Rewrite the general term:

    3n+122⁢n =3⋅3n4n
    =3⁢(34)n.

    Thus

    ∑n=0∞3n+122⁢n=∑n=0∞3⁢(34)n.

    This is geometric with

    a=3,r=34.

    Since |r|<1, the series converges. Its sum is

    31−34=12.
  11. 11.

    Example 7: Determine whether the series converges or diverges:

    ∑n=2∞(74)n+1.

    Solution. This is a geometric series with common ratio

    r=74.

    Since

    |74|>1,

    the series diverges.

  12. 12.

    Example 8: Using geometric series, write 0.08¯ as a fraction.

    Solution. We have

    0.08¯=0.0808080808⁢⋯.

    Therefore,

    0.08¯ =8102+8104+8106+⋯
    =∑n=0∞8102⁢(1102)n.

    This is geometric with

    a=8100,r=1100.

    Thus

    0.08¯=81001−1100=899.

  14. 14.

    nth Term Divergence Test

    If

    limn→∞an≠0,

    then

    ∑n=0∞an

    diverges.

    Proof

    Suppose the series

    ∑n=0∞an

    converges. Let Sn be the nth partial sum. Then the sequence of partial sums has a finite limit; say

    limn→∞Sn=S.

    Since

    Sn=a0+a1+a2+⋯+an

    and

    Sn−1=a0+a1+a2+⋯+an−1,

    we have

    an=Sn−Sn−1.

    Taking limits gives

    limn→∞an=limn→∞(Sn−Sn−1)=S−S=0.

    Therefore, if a series converges, then its terms must approach 0. Consequently, if

    limn→∞an≠0,

    the series must diverge.

    Important Note

    This test can only prove divergence. If limn→∞an=0, the test is inconclusive.

  15. 15.

    Example 10: Determine whether the following series converges or diverges:

    ∑n=1∞nn+1.

    Solution. Test for divergence first:

    limn→∞nn+1=1≠0.

    Therefore, by the nth Term Divergence Test, the series diverges.

  16. 16.

    Example 11: Determine whether the following series converges or diverges:

    ∑n=1∞n4⁢n2+1.

    Solution. Using the nth Term Divergence Test,

    limn→∞n4⁢n2+1=limn→∞14+1n2=12≠0.

    Therefore, the series diverges.

  17. 17.

    Important warning. The harmonic series

    ∑n=1∞1n

    is divergent, even though

    limn→∞1n=0.

    So if the term limit is 0, the nth Term Divergence Test tells us nothing. The series may converge or diverge.
