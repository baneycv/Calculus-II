    Motivation. We have learned a lot about integration in this course. We know, for example that is we see the following integral,

    ∫01x⁢ex2⁢𝑑x,

    we would use substitution:
    Solution:

    Let

    u=x2,d⁢u=2⁢x⁢d⁢x,

    x⁢d⁢x=12⁢d⁢u.

    x=0⟹u=0,x=1⟹u=1.

    ∫01x⁢ex2⁢𝑑x=12⁢∫01eu⁢𝑑u.

    ∫01x2⁢ex⁢𝑑x,

    ∫01ex2⁢𝑑x.

Our goal for this next unit, is to develop the tools and machinery to be able to integrate, or approximate a value of the integral to within a certain accuracy. The primary tool that we are going to use a method of function approximation. We will seek a function that we can integrate easily that is equal to f for certain values of x. There are going to be several issues that arise, as you can’t get something for nothing.

  2. 2.

    The Taylor Polynomial. Recall, a polynomial of degree n≥1 is defined as

    pn⁢(x)=a0+a1⁢x+a2⁢x2+⋯+an⁢xn.

    We seek to find an n-th degree polynomial pn⁢(x)=f⁢(x) on some interval.
    Criteria for our polynomial. We will center our polynomial around x=0. Let f⁢(x) be a continuously, infinitely differentiable function at x=0 (we can take infinitely many derivatives that are all continuous at x=0).

    1. (a)

      f⁢(0)=pn⁢(0).

    2. (b)

      f(k)⁢(0)=pn(k)⁢(0) for all positive integers k.(i.e f′⁢(0)=pn′⁢(0), f′′⁢(0)=pn′′⁢(0), etc.)

    3. (c)

      We want to find the coefficients, a0, a1, …, an, for this polynomial

  3. 3.

    Derivation. Let f be continuous for all x in some interval, and suppose further that f is infinitely differentiable, so that all derivatives are also continuous in the interval as well. Secondly, let

    pn⁢(x)=a0+a1⁢x+a2⁢x2+⋯+an⁢xn.
    1. (a)

      We want f⁢(0)=pn⁢(0). We see that

      pn⁢(0)=a0.

      Thus, we have solved for the first coefficient,

      f⁢(0)=a0.
    2. (b)

      To find the next coefficient. We enforce the second criteria that, f′⁢(0)=pn′⁢(0), f′′⁢(0)=pn′′⁢(0), etc. Observe that pn′⁢(x)=a1+2⁢a2⁢x+3⁢a3⁢x2+⋯+n⁢an⁢xn−1. We note then that pn′⁢(0)=a1. Therefore, since we want f′⁢(0)=pn′⁢(0), we find then that f′⁢(0)=a1.

    3. (c)

      We continue this way, noting that pn′′⁢(x)=2⁢a2+3⋅2⁢a3⁢x+⋯⁢n⋅(n−1)⁢an⁢xn−2. From which we see that pn⁢(0)=2⁢a2. Thus, enforcing our criteria above, this would mean then that

      f′′⁢(0) = pn′′⁢(0)⟹
      f′′⁢(0) = 2⁢a2⟹
      f′′⁢(0)2 = a2
    4. (d)

      Proceeding similarly, we find that pn′′′⁢(x)=3⋅2⁢a3+4⋅3⋅2⁢a4⁢x+⋯+n⁢(n−1)⁢(n−2)⁢an⁢xn−3. Thus, we have

      f′′′⁢(0) = pn′′′⁢(0)⟹
      f′′⁢(0) = 3⋅2⁢a3⟹
      f′′′⁢(0)3⋅2⋅1 = a3
    5. (e)

      Let’s do one more, pn(4)⁢(x)=4⋅3⋅2⋅1⁢a4+⋯⁢n⁢(n−1)⁢(n−2)⁢(n−3)⁢an⁢xn−4, so that

      f(4)⁢(0) = pn(4)⁢(0)⟹
      f(4)⁢(0) = 4⋅3⋅2⋅1⁢a4⟹
      f(4)⁢(0)4⋅3⋅2⋅1 = a4
    6. (f)

      Detour, Definition of the Factorial. Let n be any nonnegative integer. We define n! (read as “n factorial”) to be defined as follows:

      0!=11!=1,

      and

      n!=n⁢(n−1)⁢(n−2)⁢⋯⁢3⋅2⋅1.

      Example 1: Calculate 3!.
      Solution: 3!=3⋅2⋅1=6.
      Example 2: Calculate 5!.
      Solution: 5!=5⋅4⋅3⋅2⋅1=120.

    7. (g)

      We should at this point be able to guess that pn(n)⁢(x)=n⋅(n−1)⋅(n−2)⋅(n−3)⁢⋯⁢3⋅2⋅1⁢an=n!⁢an. Thus, we have that

      f(n)⁢(0) = pn(n)⁢(0)⟹
      f(n)⁢(0) = n!⁢an⟹
      f(n)⁢(0)n! = an
    8. (h)

      Putting it together. Thus, plugging in our coefficients, we obtain the following polynomial, centered at x=0.

      pn⁢(x)=f⁢(0)+f′⁢(0)⁢x+f′′⁢(0)2!⁢x2+f′′′⁢(0)3!⁢x3+⋯+f(n)⁢(0)n!⁢xn.
  4. 4.

    Observations

    1. (a)

      All of the criteria we required are fulfilled.

    2. (b)

      Note the first two terms of the polynomial, p2⁢(x)=f⁢(0)+f′⁢(0)⁢x is the equation of the line tangent to the graph of f at x=0.

    3. (c)

      We know that the polynomial and the function will equal each other at x=0 because that was required. However, we cannot conclude that they are equal to each other for any other value of x. We will explore this now.

  5. 5.

    Let f⁢(x)=ex

    1. (a)

      Determine the first degree (n=1) Taylor Polynomial, p1, of f centered x=0

      Solution:

      Since we are finding the first degree Taylor polynomial, we need terms up to degree 1. That means we need f⁢(0) and f′⁢(0).

      f⁢(x) =ex, f⁢(0) =e0=1,
      f′⁢(x) =ex, f′⁢(0) =e0=1.

      The first degree Taylor polynomial centered at x=0 is

      p1⁢(x)=f⁢(0)+f′⁢(0)⁢x.

      Substituting the values we found,

      p1⁢(x)=1+x.
    2. (b)

      Determine the fourth degree Taylor Polynomial, p4 of f centered at x=0

      Solution:

      Since we are finding the fourth degree Taylor polynomial, we need terms up to degree 4. That means we need f⁢(0),f′⁢(0),f′′⁢(0),f(3)⁢(0), and f(4)⁢(0).

      f⁢(x) =ex, f⁢(0) =e0=1,
      f′⁢(x) =ex, f′⁢(0) =e0=1,
      f′′⁢(x) =ex, f′′⁢(0) =e0=1,
      f(3)⁢(x) =ex, f(3)⁢(0) =e0=1,
      f(4)⁢(x) =ex, f(4)⁢(0) =e0=1.

      The fourth degree Taylor polynomial centered at x=0 is

      p4⁢(x)=f⁢(0)+f′⁢(0)⁢x+f′′⁢(0)2!⁢x2+f(3)⁢(0)3!⁢x3+f(4)⁢(0)4!⁢x4.

      Substituting the values we found,

      p4⁢(x)=1+x+x22!+x33!+x44!.
    3. (c)

      Determine the sixth degree Taylor Polynomial, p6 of f centered at x=0

      Solution:

      Since we are finding the sixth degree Taylor polynomial, we need terms up to degree 6. That means we need derivatives from f⁢(0) through f(6)⁢(0).

      f⁢(x) =ex, f⁢(0) =e0=1,
      f′⁢(x) =ex, f′⁢(0) =e0=1,
      f′′⁢(x) =ex, f′′⁢(0) =e0=1,
      f(3)⁢(x) =ex, f(3)⁢(0) =e0=1,
      f(4)⁢(x) =ex, f(4)⁢(0) =e0=1,
      f(5)⁢(x) =ex, f(5)⁢(0) =e0=1,
      f(6)⁢(x) =ex, f(6)⁢(0) =e0=1.

      The sixth degree Taylor polynomial centered at x=0 is

      p6⁢(x) =f⁢(0)+f′⁢(0)⁢x+f′′⁢(0)2!⁢x2+f(3)⁢(0)3!⁢x3+f(4)⁢(0)4!⁢x4
      +f(5)⁢(0)5!⁢x5+f(6)⁢(0)6!⁢x6.

      Substituting the values we found,

      p6⁢(x)=1+x+x22!+x33!+x44!+x55!+x66!.

      Determine a formula for the n-th degree term of the Taylor Polynomial centered around x=0 of f. Looking at the graphs, what are your observations?

      Solution:

      For a Taylor polynomial centered at x=0, the general formula is

      pn⁢(x)=f⁢(0)+f′⁢(0)⁢x+f′′⁢(0)2!⁢x2+⋯+f(n)⁢(0)n!⁢xn.

      f(n)⁢(x) =ex, f(n)⁢(0) =1.

    Example 2: Let f⁢(x)=ln⁡(x+1). Find the Taylor polynomial p4⁢(x) (n=4) around x=0 for f⁢(x) and a formula for the n-th degree term.
    Solution:

    Since we are finding the fourth degree Taylor polynomial, we need terms up to degree 4. That means we need f⁢(0),f′⁢(0),f′′⁢(0),f(3)⁢(0), and f(4)⁢(0).

    f⁢(x) =ln⁡(x+1), f⁢(0) =ln⁡(1)=0,
    f′⁢(x) =1x+1, f′⁢(0) =1,
    f′′⁢(x) =−1(x+1)2, f′′⁢(0) =−1,
    f(3)⁢(x) =2(x+1)3, f(3)⁢(0) =2,
    f(4)⁢(x) =−6(x+1)4, f(4)⁢(0) =−6.

    The fourth degree Taylor polynomial centered at x=0 is

    p4⁢(x)=f⁢(0)+f′⁢(0)⁢x+f′′⁢(0)2!⁢x2+f(3)⁢(0)3!⁢x3+f(4)⁢(0)4!⁢x4.

    Substituting the values we found,

    p4⁢(x)=0+x+−12!⁢x2+23!⁢x3+−64!⁢x4.

    Therefore,

    p4⁢(x)=x−x22+x33−x44.

    To find the general pattern, notice that the signs alternate and the denominator matches the power of x.

    ln⁡(1+x)=x−x22+x33−x44+⋯

    So the n-th degree term is

    an=(−1)n+1⁢xnn.

    The General Formula for the Taylor Polynomial

    The n-th degree Taylor polynomial of f⁢(x) centered at x=a is

    pn⁢(x)= f⁢(a)+f′⁢(a)⁢(x−a)+f′′⁢(a)2!⁢(x−a)2+f(3)⁢(a)3!⁢(x−a)3
    +⋯+f(n)⁢(a)n!⁢(x−a)n.

    To construct a Taylor polynomial, compute the derivatives of f, evaluate each derivative at the center a, and substitute those values into the formula above. The degree n tells you the highest power of (x−a) that appears in the polynomial.

    Example 4: Let f⁢(x)=1x. Find the Taylor polynomial p4⁢(x) around x=1 for f⁢(x) .
    Solution:

    Since we are finding the fourth degree Taylor polynomial, we need terms up to degree 4. Because the polynomial is centered at x=1, we evaluate each derivative at x=1 and use powers of (x−1).

    f⁢(x) =1x, f⁢(1) =1,
    f′⁢(x) =−1x2, f′⁢(1) =−1,
    f′′⁢(x) =2x3, f′′⁢(1) =2,
    f(3)⁢(x) =−6x4, f(3)⁢(1) =−6,
    f(4)⁢(x) =24x5, f(4)⁢(1) =24.

    The fourth degree Taylor polynomial centered at x=1 is

    p4⁢(x)= f⁢(1)+f′⁢(1)⁢(x−1)+f′′⁢(1)2!⁢(x−1)2
    +f(3)⁢(1)3!⁢(x−1)3+f(4)⁢(1)4!⁢(x−1)4.

    Substituting the values we found,

    p4⁢(x)= 1−(x−1)+22!⁢(x−1)2
    −63!⁢(x−1)3+244!⁢(x−1)4.

    Therefore,

    p4(x)=1−(x−1)+(x−1)2−(x−1)3+(x−1)4.
