Math 252, 9.5, Alternating Series Test

  1. (1)

    Alternating Series Test An Alternating Series is one in the form of ∑n=0∞(−1)n⁢an=a0−a1+a2−a3+…. The terms alternate being positive and negative.

    Theorem 1.

    (Alternating Series Test) Let an>0. The Alternating Series,

    ∑n=1∞(−1)n⁢anand∑n=1∞(−1)n+1⁢an

    converge when the two conditions listed below are met.

    1. (a)

      limn→∞an=0.

    2. (b)

      an+1≤an for all n. That is to say, {an} is decreasing.

    1. (a)

      Note, this test is for showing convergence only. In order for a series to pass the test, both conditions must be met.

    2. (b)

      If a series does not pass both conditions, we use the Nth term Divergence Test.

  2. (2)

    Example 1. Determine if ∑n=1∞(−1)n+1⁢1n converges or diverges.

    Solution. We see that the series is alternating by the presence of the alternator. Let

    an=1n.

    First,

    limn→∞1n=0.

    Next, we need to show that {an} is decreasing. We turn the sequence into the function

    f⁢(x)=1x.

    Then

    f′⁢(x)=−1x2.

    Since f′⁢(x)<0 for x≥1, the function f is decreasing on [1,∞). Therefore, the sequence an=1n is decreasing. Hence, by the Alternating Series Test, the series converges.

  3. (3)

    Example 2. Determine if ∑n=1∞n(−2)n−1 converges or diverges.

    Solution. If we write the first few terms of the series, we see that we get

    1−1+34−48+516−…

    Hence, the series is indeed alternating. The positive part of the terms is

    an=n2n−1.

    First,

    limn→∞n2n−1=0,

    because the exponential function grows faster than the linear function. Next, we need to show that {an} is decreasing. We turn the sequence into the function

    f⁢(x)=x2x−1=x⁢21−x.

    Then

    f′⁢(x)=21−x⁢(1−x⁢ln⁡2).

    Since 21−x>0, the sign of f′⁢(x) is determined by 1−x⁢ln⁡2. For x≥2,

    1−x⁢ln⁡2<0.

    Thus, f′⁢(x)<0 for x≥2, so f is decreasing on [2,∞). Therefore, the sequence an=n2n−1 is eventually decreasing. Hence, by the Alternating Series Test, the series converges.

  4. (4)

    Example 3. Determine if the series ∑n=1∞(−1)n+1⁢n+1n converges or diverges.

    Solution. We see the alternator, so we know that the series is alternating. The positive part of the terms is

    an=n+1n.

    First,

    limn→∞n+1n=limn→∞(1+1n)=1.

    Since this limit is not 0, the series fails the first condition of the Alternating Series Test. Hence, we use the Nth Term Divergence Test. Since

    limn→∞an≠0,

    we can conclude that the series diverges.

  5. (5)

    Alternating Series Remainder. For a convergent alternating series, the partial sums SN can be a useful approximation for the sum S of the series. A useful fact to know is how accurate your sum is if you truncate the terms after the Nth term. Or, conversely, we would want to know how many terms are required to get within a desired accuracy of the actual sum.

    Theorem 2.

    (Alternating Series Remainder) If a convergent alternating series satisfies the condition an+1≤an, then the absolute value of the remainder RN involved in approximating the sum S by SN is less than (or equal to) the first neglected term. That is,

    |S−SN|=|RN|≤aN+1.

    This is to say, if the first N terms are used in finding the sum of the series, the error or remainder is no bigger than the next term. This is not to say that it is exactly this value. But the bound tells us the absolute worst scenario.

  6. (6)

    Example 4. Approximate the sum of the series by the first six terms and the accuracy of the approximation.

    ∑n=1∞(−1)n+1⁢(1n!)=11!−12!+13!−14!+15!−16!+…

    Solution. The series is alternating. The positive part of the terms is

    an=1n!.

    First,

    limn→∞1n!=0.

    The sequence {an} is decreasing because the denominator gets multiplied by a larger positive integer at each step. Thus, the series satisfies the conditions of the Alternating Series Test and converges. If we add the first six terms we obtain

    S6=1−12+16−124+1120−1720≈0.63194.

    To determine the accuracy,

    |S−S6|<a7=17!=15040≈0.0002.
  7. (7)

    Example 5. Determine the number of terms required to approximate the sum of the series to within 0.001 of the actual sum,

    ∑n=1∞(−1)n+1n4.

    Solution. The series is alternating. The positive part of the terms is

    an=1n4.

    First,

    limn→∞1n4=0.

    Next, we need to show that {an} is decreasing. We turn the sequence into the function

    f⁢(x)=1x4=x−4.

    Then

    f′⁢(x)=−4⁢x−5=−4x5.

    Since f′⁢(x)<0 for x≥1, the function f is decreasing on [1,∞). Therefore, the sequence an=1n4 is decreasing, and the Alternating Series Remainder applies. We have that

    |RN|≤aN+1=1(N+1)4.

    Therefore,

    1(N+1)4<0.001⟹(N+1)4>1000⟹N>10004−1≈4.6.

    Thus, if we use the first 5 terms of the series to approximate the sum, we would be within 0.001 of the actual answer.

  8. (8)

    Absolute and Conditional Convergence We sometimes come across series that do not alternate, but have positive and negative terms. For example,

    ∑n=1∞sin⁡nn2=sin⁡11+sin⁡24+sin⁡39+sin⁡416+…
    Theorem 3.

    (Absolute Convergence) If the series ∑|an| converges, then the series ∑an also converges.

    1. (a)

      The converse of this theorem is not true. For example, we know that ∑n=1∞(−1)n+1⁢1n is the Alternating Harmonic Series and we showed that it converges. However, the positive series diverges. This type of series is referred to as Conditionally Convergent.

    2. (b)

      If ∑|an| converges, then ∑an is said to be absolutely convergent.

  9. (9)

    Example 6. Determine if the series converges, and then classify the convergence,

    ∑n=1∞sin⁡nn2.

    Solution. We see that

    |sin⁡nn2|≤1n2.

    Hence,

    ∑n=1∞|sin⁡nn2|≤∑n=1∞1n2.

    Thus, by the Comparison Test, ∑n=1∞|sin⁡nn2| converges. Since ∑|an| converges, our series is absolutely convergent.

  10. (10)

    Example 7. Determine if the series converges, and then classify the convergence,

    ∑n=1∞(−1)nn.

    Solution. We see that the series is alternating. The positive part of the terms is

    an=1n.

    First,

    limn→∞1n=0.

    Next, we need to show that {an} is decreasing. We turn the sequence into the function

    f⁢(x)=1x=x−1/2.

    Then

    f′⁢(x)=−12⁢x−3/2=−12⁢x3/2.

    Since f′⁢(x)<0 for x≥1, the function f is decreasing on [1,∞). Therefore, the sequence an=1n is decreasing. By the Alternating Series Test, the series converges. However, we know that

    ∑n=1∞|(−1)nn|=∑n=1∞1n,

    which is a p-series, where p=1/2<1, thus it diverges. So our series is conditionally convergent.
