    Derivation of Derivative Formulas

    Suppose that a parametric curve is given by

    x=f⁢(t)andy=g⁢(t).

    Consider the points on the curve corresponding to the parameter values t and t+Δ⁢t:

    P=(f⁢(t),g⁢(t))andQ=(f⁢(t+Δ⁢t),g⁢(t+Δ⁢t)).

    The slope of the secant line through P and Q is

    Δ⁢yΔ⁢x = g⁢(t+Δ⁢t)−g⁢(t)f⁢(t+Δ⁢t)−f⁢(t)
    = g⁢(t+Δ⁢t)−g⁢(t)Δ⁢tf⁢(t+Δ⁢t)−f⁢(t)Δ⁢t.

    As Δ⁢t⟶0, the point Q approaches the point P, and the secant line approaches the tangent line. Therefore,

    d⁢yd⁢x = limΔ⁢t→0g⁢(t+Δ⁢t)−g⁢(t)Δ⁢tf⁢(t+Δ⁢t)−f⁢(t)Δ⁢t
    = limΔ⁢t→0g⁢(t+Δ⁢t)−g⁢(t)Δ⁢tlimΔ⁢t→0f⁢(t+Δ⁢t)−f⁢(t)Δ⁢t
    = g′⁢(t)f′⁢(t)
    = d⁢y/d⁢td⁢x/d⁢t,

    provided d⁢xd⁢t≠0.

    Higher Order Derivatives.

    We know that

    d⁢yd⁢x=d⁢y/d⁢td⁢x/d⁢t.

    To find the second derivative, differentiate the first derivative with respect to x:

    d2⁢yd⁢x2=dd⁢x⁢(d⁢yd⁢x).

    Using the first derivative formula with

    y=d⁢yd⁢x,

    we obtain

    d2⁢yd⁢x2=dd⁢t⁢(d⁢yd⁢x)d⁢xd⁢t.

    Since

    d⁢yd⁢x=d⁢y/d⁢td⁢x/d⁢t,

    it follows that

    d2⁢yd⁢x2=dd⁢t⁢(d⁢y/d⁢td⁢x/d⁢t)d⁢xd⁢t.

    Example 2: For the curve x=t and y=14⁢(t2−4), for t≥0, find the slope and concavity at the point (2,3).

    At the point (2,3),

    2=t,

    so

    t=4.

    Now,

    d⁢xd⁢t=12⁢tandd⁢yd⁢t=t2.

    Thus,

    d⁢yd⁢x = d⁢y/d⁢td⁢x/d⁢t
    = t/21/(2⁢t)
    = t⁢t.

    At t=4,

    d⁢yd⁢x=4⁢4=8.

    Therefore, the slope at (2,3) is 8.

    For concavity,

    d2⁢yd⁢x2 = dd⁢t⁢(d⁢yd⁢x)d⁢xd⁢t
    = dd⁢t⁢(t3/2)1/(2⁢t)
    = 32⁢t1/(2⁢t)
    = 3⁢t.

    At t=4,

    d2⁢yd⁢x2=12>0.

    Therefore, the curve is concave upward at (2,3).

    Arc Length Consider a curve C whose equation is given by y=h⁢(x). If we want to find the length of the arc between x0 and x1, recall we have the formiula

    s = ∫x0x11+[h′⁢(x)]2⁢𝑑x

    d⁢yd⁢x=d⁢y/d⁢td⁢x/d⁢tandd⁢x=d⁢xd⁢t⁢d⁢t,

    Example 4: A circle of radius 1 rolls around the circumference of a large circle of radius 4. Determine the distance traveled by the point in one complete trip around the larger circle.

    One complete trip around the larger circle occurs for

    0≤t≤2⁢π.

    The epicycloid has four-fold rotational symmetry. The portion of the curve traced for

    0≤t≤π2

    has the same length as each of the other three portions of the curve. Therefore, we find the length of this portion and multiply by 4.

    We have

    d⁢xd⁢t=−5⁢sin⁡t+5⁢sin⁡5⁢t

    and

    d⁢yd⁢t=5⁢cos⁡t−5⁢cos⁡5⁢t.

    s = 4⁢∫0π/2(−5⁢sin⁡t+5⁢sin⁡5⁢t)2+(5⁢cos⁡t−5⁢cos⁡5⁢t)2⁢𝑑t
    = 20⁢∫0π/2(sin⁡5⁢t−sin⁡t)2+(cos⁡t−cos⁡5⁢t)2⁢𝑑t

    0≤t≤π2,

    we have

    0≤2⁢t≤π,

    4⁢sin2⁡2⁢t=2⁢sin⁡2⁢t.
